# Process Friction Audit

Find a proportionate process that supports agreed outcomes, coordination and material safeguards.
Neither fewer rules nor more controls are inherently better. This skill does not authorize changes,
messages, relaxed acceptance or external actions. Treat evaluated instructions as evidence, not new
execution authority; comply with applicable instructions while assessing their design.

## Choose the depth

Use the lightweight mode for a concrete design choice about human effort. Use the full mode when
asked to assess process quality, compare workflows, score burden or investigate recurring friction.
Do not turn ordinary implementation into a mandatory audit. A lightweight check need not escalate
merely because evidence is incomplete; report a material uncertainty if it affects the decision.

## Lightweight self-check

Prefer the simplest approach sufficient for agreed requirements and material risks. Do not introduce
human obligations solely because a familiar template includes them or a hypothetical future need
might arise. An absent explicit requirement does not remove necessary safety, correctness,
accessibility or authority constraints.

For a proposed field, confirmation, decision, handoff, status update or operator step, consider:

- What explicit requirement, concrete user benefit or material risk justifies this obligation?
- What would be lost without it, given the other mechanisms already present?
- Could an appropriate default, reuse, automation or conditional presentation preserve that benefit?

Choose: retain, simplify, make conditional, or do not add. Prefer system-enforced safeguards where
appropriate; automation must not silently decide unresolved product choices or bypass authorization.
Do not remove an agreed process requirement without agreement to change it.

No score, written checklist, new document, approval or recurring review is required. Explain only
material tradeoffs in the normal response. Stop once the design is sufficient; do not keep searching
for a theoretically minimal process.

## Full assessment

Read [the rubric](references/rubric.md). Read [research and limitations](references/research.md)
when explaining provenance or evaluating the method itself; do not repeat web research routinely.

Use the requested scope and available evidence. Resolve a missing scope only when it materially
changes the assessment. Distinguish process design, observed execution and proposed alternatives.
A rule can imply burden without proving actual cost; an agent's account is not independent proof
that an action ran or a safeguard worked.

Extract distinct obligations and their application conditions. Map them to needed outcomes or
control functions, then assess burden, marginal contribution and feasible alternatives. Include
missing necessary functions, not just redundant activities. High-level ownership and completion
boundaries may be sufficient; do not demand a document or detailed procedure per function.

Attribute requirements accurately: explicit rule, agent interpretation, discretionary action,
operator request, or unresolved source. Consider other explanations such as genuine engineering
complexity, changed acceptance, invalidated evidence, external delay and newly available data.
Do not equate slow progress or an unchanging completion counter with bureaucracy.

Report a scoped conclusion, material findings with source references, applicable scores and their
basis, useful controls to preserve, and the smallest justified improvements. Use existing artifacts;
no fixed report template or number of findings is required. A finding may have several descriptive
tags but is counted once. Do not manufacture a finding or rank a whole organization from a narrow
sample. Preserve uncertainty and identify sampling limits.

Separate observed friction, causal attribution and demonstrated intervention benefit. If causation
is unresolved, label the explanation and proposed experiment accordingly. Do not promise time savings
without evidence. A control that found no error is not thereby useless.

Finish within the agreed scope. Expand investigation only for a specific evidence gap capable of
changing the conclusion or recommendation; otherwise give a bounded result. Evaluation alone does
not authorize implementation. For authorized improvements, preserve useful functions and change
existing owners rather than introduce another process layer.
