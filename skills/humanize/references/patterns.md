# Humanization pattern catalog

Use these as diagnostics, not mechanical bans. A pattern matters when it obscures evidence, flattens
the intended voice, or repeats enough to make the passage feel assembled. Preserve legitimate genre
conventions and exact technical language.

## Claims and evidence

| Pattern | Diagnostic signal | Better direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Significance inflation | Routine facts become pivotal moments, testaments, or broad trends. | State the event and its supported consequence. |
| Notability padding | Lists of media names or credentials replace the relevant claim. | Cite the specific coverage or argument that matters. |
| Promotional language | Vibrant, groundbreaking, renowned, seamless, or stunning appears without evidence. | Name the concrete property or outcome. |
| Vague attribution | Experts, observers, reports, or critics appear without identifiable sources. | Name the source or narrow/remove the attribution. |
| Unsupported synthesis | An `-ing` clause adds importance, symbolism, or causality not established by the source. | Separate the observation from any supported interpretation. |
| Knowledge disclaimers | Training-cutoff or availability language survives inside publishable prose. | Use a sourced date/fact or state the exact uncertainty. |
| Excessive hedging | Several qualifiers stack around one claim. | Keep the one qualifier that matches the evidence. |
| Generic conclusion | The future is bright or a journey continues without a concrete next state. | End on a fact, decision, consequence, or open question. |
| Formulaic outlook | A generic challenges/future section could apply to any subject. | Describe only evidenced constraints and planned actions. |
| Premature slogan | An abstract verdict arrives before the situation that supports it. | Show the source-backed action or state first. |

## Sentence and rhetorical construction

| Pattern | Diagnostic signal | Better direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Copula avoidance | `Serves as`, `stands as`, `features`, or `boasts` replaces a clearer `is` or `has`. | Use the direct construction when it is more precise. |
| Negative parallelism | Repeated `not just X, but Y` supplies drama rather than distinction. | State the actual relationship directly. |
| Forced rule of three | Ideas are padded or omitted to fit three-part symmetry. | Use the number of points the material supports. |
| Synonym cycling | The same entity receives new labels in adjacent sentences. | Repeat the precise noun when clarity benefits. |
| False range | `From X to Y` joins items that are not endpoints on one scale. | List or group the actual topics. |
| Hidden actor | Passive voice or fragments conceal who acts or owns the result. | Name the actor when known and relevant. |
| Filler phrase | `In order to`, `at this point in time`, or similar wording delays the claim. | Use the shorter equivalent. |
| Persuasive authority trope | `The real question`, `at its core`, or `what matters` announces importance. | Ask or state the concrete question. |
| Signposting | `Let's dive in` or `here is what you need to know` announces the next paragraph. | Begin with the substance. |
| Fragmented header | A heading is followed by a sentence that merely restates it. | Remove the warm-up or merge it with the real paragraph. |
| Repetitive rhythm | Paragraphs share the same sentence length and grammatical shape. | Recast only enough sentences to fit the natural flow. |

## Formatting and correspondence leakage

| Pattern | Diagnostic signal | Better direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mechanical emphasis | Most list items or key nouns are bold without a navigation need. | Keep emphasis only where it aids scanning. |
| Inline-header list | Every bullet starts with a bold label and colon despite being ordinary prose. | Use plain bullets or a paragraph when structure adds little. |
| Heading-title uniformity | Every heading uses promotional title case regardless of local convention. | Follow the document's established heading style. |
| Decorative emoji | Emoji label routine sections without carrying meaning. | Remove them unless the product voice uses them deliberately. |
| Punctuation tic | Em dashes, semicolons, parentheses, or clipped fragments recur as a manufactured voice. | Mix constructions only where syntax benefits. |
| Chatbot artifact | `Great question`, `I hope this helps`, or `let me know` appears in the deliverable. | Remove correspondence that is not part of the content. |
| Sycophancy | Praise of the reader or their question delays the answer. | Address the substance directly. |
| Repeated CTA architecture | Hook, title, labels, and close all repeat the same slogan. | Give each surface the information its role needs. |

## Material-grounding test

For a passage that still feels synthetic after surface cleanup, ask:

1. Who acts or decides?
2. What do they do?
3. What object, input, or record do they act on?
4. What changes or appears?
5. What differs from the expected or supplied material?
6. What source, state, or observation lets the reader verify that difference?

Use only fields supported by the source. Technical prose can ground a claim in a command, input,
output, state transition, trace, or source record; it does not need a cinematic scene.

## False positives

- Brevity, abstraction, passive voice, parallelism, and lists can all be appropriate.
- Exact quotations, legal language, API names, and established product copy are not style debris.
- Repetition can improve technical clarity and accessibility.
- Neutrality is not soulless when the genre requires it.
- A personal voice must come from the author or supplied sample, never from invented experience.
